# Round 1: Claude's pitches (the fourth voice)

## The root of "only kiting"

Two things, not one. The slots force every build into the same hybrid (one ranged, one
area, one melee, one movement), and **the auto attack is ranged**, so standing back is
free damage that no build gives up. Enemies commit and walking away always answers them.
Change the slots and leave the auto, and it's still kiting. So my first pitch is about the
auto.

## B. Playstyles

**C-B1. The auto follows the arms** (*surprising*). The auto attack stops being a fixed
5-damage shot at 7.6 u and becomes whatever the arms part says: a Scrap Cleaver auto is a
short, heavier swipe at ~2.2 u; a Rusted Hook auto tugs; a ranged-leaning arms part keeps
the shot. The auto is most of the damage between cooldowns, so where your auto reaches is
where you want to stand. That single rule makes a close brawler real without magnetism,
and keeps the stick as the only way in. *Cost:* medium (the auto becomes a per-part field;
each of the arms parts gets an auto line).

**C-B2. Keep the body, add leanings.** Slots stay role-locked: Still *is* a body, and
wearing parts per slot is the game's picture of him (and the art, and the wall). But every
part carries one leaning (Brawler, Marksman, Caster, Keeper, Tempo), and wearing **two or
more** of a leaning wakes a behaviour, never a stat:
- **Brawler:** a hit landed within 3 u shortens your other cooldowns a little.
- **Marksman:** standing still for 0.6 s steadies you: the next bolt pierces.
- **Caster:** a nova leaves its shape on the floor for 2 s (burning, slowing or pulling).
- **Keeper (summoner):** decoys and drones last longer and act on their own.
- **Tempo (the masher):** casting three different slots within 2 s makes the third free of
  cooldown once.
Four slots, so a run can lean fully one way, or split two and two. *Cost:* medium (a tag on
each part, five behaviours, a small mark on the card and the pause screen).

**C-B3. Parts to fill the gaps.** A **drone** head part (orbits, fires at the nearest on
its own, so auto-aim suits it perfectly) and a **turret** torso part for the Keeper; a
**flurry** arms part (0.6 s cooldown, small hits, builds Tempo) for the masher. *Cost:*
medium, three parts.

## A. Enemy variety

**C-A1. Rosters, families, champions.** Each level draws 3 archetypes from its depth's pool
instead of a fixed list; each archetype gets 2-3 named variants from the old roster (same
behaviour, one twist, own body tone); and one pack per level is led by a **named champion**
with two stacked modifiers from a list of ~10 (splits on death, leaves slag, shields its
pack, blinks, hastes others…). *Cost:* cheap for rosters, medium for the rest.

**C-A2. The mender.** A support that repairs a fallen machine next to it (a visible 1.5 s
wind-up over the wreck). Kill it first, or break the repair with a push. *Cost:* medium.

**C-A3. Meet the thief.** Depths 1-2 and 4-5, and it steals from an opened crate's drop as
well as a part left on the floor, so it acts in most runs. *Cost:* cheap.

## C. Where they meet

Champions and variants each lean against one playstyle, and a level's roster shows it at
the door: shields from the front (flank them: brawler), a mender hanging back (reach it:
marksman), swarms (area: caster), snipers that out-range you (bodies in the way: keeper).
A run then asks for a build, and the loot you find lets you answer it.

## Bundle
C-B1 (the auto follows the arms) + C-B2 (leanings) + C-A1 (rosters and champions), with
C-A3 (the thief) as a cheap extra.
