# The build layer: what we'd build

1 Oct 2026. Four voices (balancer, translator, verifier, Claude), two rounds. The brief is `BRIEF.md` (with its
Corrections), the raw rounds are `1-*.md` and `2-*.md`, and the numbers come from `build-sim.mjs`. Every name and every
player-facing line here is PLACEHOLDER: yours to write.

Your verdict was: hollow, easy, parts too alike and too few, no build, and leanings that are forced because the hand and
the eye are "the same verb at two lengths". Your answers for the round: everything open; a build should change what parts
do together and how you fight.

## The short answer

**Every Still plays the same today, so no part can make a run feel like a build.** The fix all four reached on their own:

> **One core a run replaces the hand and the eye. The core is a new way to fight, and it marks enemies on every beat.
> Your parts spend those marks.**

The clock is why. A part fires once or twice in a 5-8 s pack fight, so a part that sets something up and another that
pays it off meet about once a fight: today's chill pair measures at **-4% on packs**. The auto fires every 0.62 s. Only
the auto can be the thing that sets up.

## Agreed by all four

- **The core.** A fifth piece with no button. It is the auto: with a core worn there is no close strike and no planted
  shot. One core a run. The four-button arc stays exactly as it was thumb-tested.
- **Marks.** The core leaves marks on what it touches (a ring at the body's feet, filling in thirds). Marks last 2-4 s.
- **Spending is the bottleneck, not setting up.** A core leaves 6-15 marks in a deep pack fight; one spender uses 2-8. So
  the parts worth hunting are **spenders** (cash marks), **shapers** (move bodies so a spend catches more) and
  **keystones** (change the core itself). A part that only adds marks is worth ~0 (+0-4%), so that role is cut.
- **The button shows a number**: how many marks this cast would spend now. At 3 or more it pulses when ready, and the
  push cue lights when cooling. That gives the push a clear job: the marks are there and the button isn't.
- **Melting is too strong, and it has to come down in the same trial.** All four parts at rank III is +27-37% on packs and
  +68-84% on a boss today. No build at rank I comes close, so "melt everything" stays the best build: the deckbuilder's
  stat-stacking failure, back as ranks. Flatter temper (damage x1.15 / x1.3, cooldown x0.92 / x0.85) makes building and
  melting peers; doing both lands where today's all-III player is.
- **The hunt without pedestals** (you turned them off on 28 Sep): 0.4-0.5 of the elite, Plenty and Assembler drops are drawn
  from your core's parts. Live drops with no filter form a build by the Assembler only 54% of the time, and your keystone is
  seen by d6 in 21% of runs (an always-take when it shows). With the filter: 68-75% formed.
- **Cut:** the lean tags (they steer nothing), the rider code (dead since 28 Sep), mastery as it is (the core does its job
  from d1). **Keep:** the four slots, temper (flattened), the states machinery, shatter, strain and tap push, weight.
- **Scrap** (my idea: kills drop scrap that parts spend): **not in the trial.** It arrives in the back half of a fight,
  a boss leaves none, and as a shield it helps every build equally. Later, maybe as one core's own idea.
- **Measure first.** The drop simulator still models 6 depths with pedestals on, and the check suites pin weight and tap push
  off. Both have to describe the game you play before any build number counts.

## The first trial: Wake, plus one

**Wake is in.** Three of four proposed it, and it's the strongest pack build the sim can price.
- Wake marks only what Still **passes beside**: within 1.6 u, moving sideways to it, never straight away. Once per body per
  second. The core itself does almost no damage, and every Wake spender is close.
- That rule is there because of 26 Sep: the free auto made running away the only way to play. A Wake that marks bodies
  following you would pay you for retreating again. "Beside, not behind" means Wake has to cut through the danger.
- Log line that says it failed: Wake's median distance to the nearest enemy above 3.5 u, or Wake clearing packs 20% faster
  than the other core.

**The second core is split three ways.** Each candidate has a real objection:

| | what it is | the case for | the case against |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Ram** | the core shoves the nearest body 1.5 u; a body that hits a wall or another body is slammed (marked) | neither core is a hit at a range, so it passes your "two lengths" test; Still holds ground and enemies move, the opposite of Wake; a boss can't be moved, so every shove on one slams | its pack value depends on how often a shove ends on something, which the sim can't see (bosses: +55%); the verifier calls a shove every beat free defence (the translator: a hulk walks the 1.5 u back in 0.35 s) |
| **Breaker** | the core snaps automatically when something in reach starts to swing; the body is left open | the strongest fit to "the enemy causes it"; reuses today's reel | the sim has it failing on packs (+3-11% with a full build); the translator now calls it "today's hand on a better timer"; it can't cancel a pressure tell, so it's a counter-punch, not a block |
| **Sight** | today's planted shot alone, marking every body it hits | the cheapest to build; passes the sim on packs and bosses (+21% / +38%) | it's the eye. Three voices think your first Sight run says "that's the eye again" |

**My recommendation: Ram + Wake.** Ram is the only candidate that passes your test and isn't failed by the numbers: its
pack value is unknown, not bad, and the trial measures exactly that (slams per shove). Breaker's catch isn't lost: it
becomes Ram's boss keystone ("the shove fires the moment something in reach winds up"). Sight stays the cheap fallback.

## When you pick the core

Split two and two. **My recommendation: at the run's start, from two cards** (balancer and translator). Picking after d1
costs 6-13 points of "formed by the Assembler", d1's parts get chosen blind, and every run starts by learning a kit you're
about to lose. The verifier's worry (the checks boot at d1 and should test the real game) is solved by letting a check boot
with a core. The control is the pause switch: off is today's game.

## Settled by the lead (where the rounds split)

1. **A spend adds flat damage per mark, not x2.** The balancer, translator and I against the verifier. x2 multiplies with
   temper (a melted part's every pay goes up) and doubles area parts on every marked body: Wake's build goes from +26% to
   +50%, past the band. The card says "+X each".
2. **The control is the pause switch "builds", on by default** (your rule: no finished work hidden). Off is today's game.
3. **Something left to want after d6 is the weakest part of the plan.** No proposal passes in the sim: a part is taken after
   d6 in 8-30% of runs (the line is half). The two ideas with a mechanism: a **second keystone per core** (one for packs, one
   for bosses) that swaps with the first in the core's one socket, and **core upgrades** from melting past III, at most two,
   each one changing what you see on the floor, not a number. Both go in the trial; the late second core (offered after the
   Arbiter) waits.

## What it would cost you

- **The game gets harder by default.** Every core alone is weaker than today's hand + eye (6-31% slower kills). You said easy,
  so I'd accept it. Builds then win it back. The never-melt player's 1 in 5 will move; the threat round re-sets it.
- **Bosses get easier for boss-leaning builds** (Ram +55%). You already find two of them too easy. The threat round, straight
  after, tunes them against these builds, not against today.
- **Content:** ~6 parts and 2 keystones per core, mostly today's parts with a new job; a few reshaped (Backhand, Skate,
  Frost Flare). Not thirty new parts: more parts before the frame works would repeat "archetypes on paper only".
