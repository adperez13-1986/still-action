# Build layer, round 1: translator

1 Oct 2026. Written alone, before reading the other voices.

Every new name below (cores, states, new parts) and every quoted player-facing line is PLACEHOLDER, his to write.
Old part names are the ones in `src/abilities.ts`. Numbers are my proposals to the balancer, not settled values.

## My position in six lines

1. A build is a **core**. It is a fifth piece with no button, and it decides what Still does when no thumb is
   pressing. It replaces the hand and the eye. One core is worn at a time.
2. Each core puts **one state on enemies all the time**, and it is caused by what the enemy does. Parts **cash it**.
   The cashers in your slots, and the piece that covers what your core can't do, are what you hunt.
3. Three cores, three different jobs for the left thumb: **keep moving** (Wake), **step into them** (Breaker),
   **stand still and line them up** (Line).
4. The four buttons stay as they are, and each slot gets a job: arms cash, head covers your blind spot, torso
   shapes the room, legs set your footwork.
5. The missing piece shows in four places. A glow on the pedestal. One line on the card. A lit glyph on the button.
   A one-sentence build readout on the pause screen.
6. First trial: two cores (Wake and Breaker) plus about 5 fitting parts each. The bare start stays today's game, so
   one run can hold both "before" and "after".

## 1. What is a build in Still?

**The unit he commits to is the core** (PLACEHOLDER word). It is the light in Still's open cage torso, made into a
piece he finds.

- A core is the auto. While it is worn, there is no close strike and no planted shot. The core does its own thing.
- A core has no button. The four-button arc stays exactly as it was thumb-tested.
- A core sets **one state**, all the time, from the first second of a fight. The synergy round found that
  setups which wait on a 5-8 s cooldown are worth nothing in a 4-6 s pack fight, while a state set by the auto is
  worth about a rank (mastery). The core moves that engine from "after rank III" to depth 1.
- Parts **cash** the state: a hit on a body carrying it does x2 and uses it up (today's `STATE`, cap x2, unchanged).
- Still starts with the **bare core**: today's hand and eye, unchanged. A player who never takes a core plays
  today's game. That is the mixed fallback, and it is the control for the trial.

**How he knows by depth 2-3:**

- d1: the exit pedestals always hold one core. Taking it changes his body and how he moves right away.
- d2: the core's state is on every pack. Some buttons light when it is live, and some don't. The buttons that don't
  light are the question.
- d3: the pause screen's readout names the gap, e.g. "Rime Core chills. Scrap Cleaver cashes it. Nothing reaches
  what won't chase." (PLACEHOLDER). That gap is "what it wants next".

## 2. The pieces

### Roles, not tags

Every part has one of four roles for each core: **cashes**, **covers**, **moves**, or **plain**. "Plain" means it
does its own job with no tie to the core (rule 4: weaker off-build, never dead). The card says the role in one line,
and only for the core he wears.

| slot | job in every build | why the slot is never dead (rule 5) |
|---|---|---|
| arms | the main cash: the hit that spends the state | where most of the payoff lives |
| head | covers the enemy your core can't reach | each core has one blind spot, and head fixes it |
| torso | shapes the room: pull, push, shield | each core needs a different shape of crowd |
| legs | the footwork the core wants | each core moves differently, so each wants different legs |

### Counts

- **3 cores** (plus the bare core). Each has **8-10 fitting parts**, and about a third of those fit two cores
  (bridges). Bridges are what make a mixed loadout work.
- **Pool: about 30 parts**, today's count, mostly today's parts with new jobs. About 5 are reshaped or new. The
  mapping is in section 4.
- **Picks a run:** about 6 exit sets, 1-2 boss gifts and 0-2 Plenty, plus floor drops. So 8-10 real choices, and he
  sees about half of a core's fits. Two Wake runs come out different (rule 9).

### How a build forms inside 9 depths

| depth | what happens | what he feels |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | bare core + one random white. The exit set: one core + two parts | "do I change how I fight?" |
| 2 | with a core worn, one exit pedestal in three is drawn from its fits (today's `LEAN_MATCH`, keyed to the core) | "that one glows" |
| 3 | Assembler. The gift set holds a gold that fits | first boss with a chain |
| 4-5 | a second core may stand in a set or at Plenty | the moth and the flame |
| 6-9 | the last cover piece is hunted. Duplicates melt into what he wears | the build never quite finishes |

### Swapping a core

Taking a new core sends the old one **back to the wall**, gone for the run. It is not left on the floor. The card
shows what goes dark before he decides (section 5). The loss has to be real, or swapping costs nothing.

## 3. How you fight: three builds, in the hands

Each sketch is one fight at d2: three hulks and one sentinel, played turn by turn on the phone.

### Build A: Wake (PLACEHOLDER), "never stop"

**Rime Core** (PLACEHOLDER, new): "Moving leaves a cold wake. Whatever walks on it is chilled. Stand still and
you do nothing." (PLACEHOLDER)
- Numbers: the wake is 1.2 u wide and lasts 2.5 s. At 5.5 u/s that is a tail about 14 u long. A body that crosses it
  takes 3 damage (at most once a second) and is chilled for 2 s. No slow: a free auto may not protect him on its own
  (the synergy round's rule, kept).
- **Why it is the enemy's doing:** chasers chase. They chill themselves by following him.

Turn by turn:
1. The pack wakes. **Left thumb:** he swings wide and starts a loop around the pack. A pale ribbon unrolls behind
   Still.
2. The hulks follow him onto the ribbon. Rime creeps up their legs (today's rime). It takes under a second.
3. The Scrap Cleaver's button shows the frost glyph, and it **lights**: chilled bodies are in reach.
4. **Left thumb reverses for one beat**, turning into them. **Right thumb:** Cleaver. Three chilled bodies take x2.
   The rime breaks with the shared "paid" sound. Then the stick sweeps back into the loop.
5. The sentinel holds at 8 u and never walks on the ice. That is the blind spot. With **Frost Flare** (head), he lobs
   a patch of wake under it. Without it, he has to run the loop past its feet and eat a shot.
6. With **Backhand** (arms), step 4 has no turn: the swing goes the way he came, and he never stops. That is the
   piece a Wake player hunts.

**What he watches:** the ribbon behind him and the rime on their legs. Never his own target.

**From 3 m away:** a pale looping scribble on the floor, Still never still, white shards bursting off a knot of
bodies.

**The risk, said plainly:** this is kiting, the thing the hand was built to stop (26 Sep). The old failure was that
kiting was free and won for every build. Here it is one core's verb, and it costs something: no damage while
standing, no answer to shooters without a cover piece, and the cash means turning back into them. If Wake wins every
run, Backhand is the first dial (a longer cooldown), not the wake.

### Build B: Breaker (PLACEHOLDER), "step in"

**Catch Core** (PLACEHOLDER, new): "When something in reach starts to hit you, you snap at it first. What you
catch is left open." (PLACEHOLDER)
- Numbers: reach 3.0 u, outside a hulk's 2.4 slam. It fires the moment a tell or windup starts (not on the auto's
  0.62 s beat), at most once a second, for 6 damage. The body is **open** (PLACEHOLDER) for 1.5 s. Between snaps,
  a plain 5-damage strike every 1.2 s, so the core is never dead.
- **Open** uses today's reel: the body staggers back and its core pulses hot amber (`REEL`, `reelCore`). It is the
  enemy's own light, so the colour rule holds. Cashers do x2 on an open body and use it up.
- **Why it is the enemy's doing:** nothing opens until they swing. Under the pressure rule they swing often, but the
  tells are 120-260 ms. A thumb can't catch that reliably on a phone, so the core catches it, and his job is to
  **be in reach when it comes**.
- **The cap is the design.** One snap a second means four hulks around him still land hits. So the build wants them
  one or two at a time, at the edge of the pack. That is a positioning rule he will feel within two fights.

Turn by turn:
1. The pack wakes. **Left thumb:** short pushes toward the nearest hulk, to the edge of the pack, never the middle.
2. A hulk cocks. **Snap:** a short cold crack, a heavy click, the hulk reels with its core pulsing amber.
3. Piston's button shows the open glyph and lights. **Right thumb:** Piston, within 1.5 s. x2, and the hulk flies 4 u
   back into its packmates.
4. The second hulk swings while the snap is still cooling. Parry Clamp (if worn) is the second catch. If it isn't,
   he takes the jab. The cap is the threat.
5. The sentinel at 9 u is out of reach. That is the blind spot. **Rusted Hook** drags it into reach, where it raises
   its lens and gets snapped. **Lunge** (legs) steps him to it.

**What he watches:** their tells, and the amber pulse that says "now".

**From 3 m away:** Still in the thick of it, short bright cracks, bodies staggering back in a ring.

## 4. Keep / cut / reshape

| system | call | one-line reason, tied to sections 1-3 |
|---|---|---|
| four slots as buttons | **keep**, with jobs | the arc is thumb-tested. Jobs (cash, cover, shape, move) stop dead slots |
| the hand and the eye | **reshape** | always-on is what flattens builds. The pair becomes the bare core, and the eye becomes the Sight Core |
| leanings (close / marksman tags) | **cut** | he called them forced. "Fits your core" replaces them, because it says what a part does with the rest, and a tag never did |
| riders | **cut** (already cut in code on 28 Sep; the brief still lists them) | the core's state does the job, the enemy-caused trigger, every second instead of rarely |
| states | **keep** chilled and marked, **add** open (from reel), **hold** slammed, **cut** hauled | one state per core, so "lit" always means one thing |
| shatter | **keep** if built | it is what makes a cash count on packs |
| temper / melting | **keep** | the wrong part still has a use, and the floor player's growth stays. Cores don't melt (too rare to duplicate) |
| mastery | **cut** | its job, the auto setting a state, is now the core, from d1 instead of after rank III |
| pedestals | **keep**, match on, keyed to the core | the pedestal is where the hunt happens. d1 exit always holds one core |
| weight's pack HP (x1.4-1.65) | **keep** | fights must last past set-then-cash. 4-6 s is enough when the core sets in the first second |
| weight's part damage | **keep for now**, balancer to recheck | a x2 cash on top of the heavier numbers may delete packs. That is the balancer's sim |
| follow-through bank | **cut for cores**, keep for the bare core | a wake and a snap can't wait for banked beats. A reaction that waits is not a reaction |
| strain and the push | **keep** (his call) | the push gets a clear job: push a cashing button the moment its glyph lights |

## 5. Card, drop and HUD

Kept to what the thumb and eye need mid-fight. Everything else goes on the pause and compare screens.

### In the fight (HUD)

- **Cashing glyph on the button.** Today's push cue (`STATE_GLYPH` on the payer's rim), widened: it shows on every
  part that cashes your core's state, **dim** when nothing is in reach, **lit** when the state is live on a body
  this cast would hit, and a soft pulse when it is lit and ready. One rule: lit = now.
- **No new meter, no core button.** The core shows on Still's body (the cage light takes the core's shape: a crystal,
  a plate, a ring) and in its floor mark (the ribbon, the snap crack, the sightline).
- **States on bodies:** rime (chilled, exists), brackets (marked, exist), reel pulse (open, exists). Nothing new
  to learn except the ribbon.

  from across the room.
- A fitting floor drop shows the same small glyph above it.

## 6. Where I disagree

1. **With LESSONS rule 1 (two leanings first).** I want three cores. The rule guarded against archetypes on paper
   only (Still shipped four heat styles with one supported). Two builds are a binary, A or not-A, and he has just
   called the two we have forced. My guard instead: no core ships without its 6+ fits, and the trial ships two.
2. **With the leanings agreement, "the hand and the eye never change with what you wear".** That line is why every
   build plays the same in the hands. The auto has to be the build.
3. **With the brief's summary:** riders are already cut in `src/abilities.ts` (28 Sep comment, no part carries one).
   The verifier should confirm the brief is out of date.
4. **With his hope that "how strong you get" follows on its own.** It won't. A chain that works makes runs easier
   again, and he already says easy. The threat round has to come straight after, tuned against these builds. I would
   not let this round's trial be judged on "harder".

**Pass lines from the log (6+ runs, his feel asked first):**
- P1 **He takes a core:** by d2 in 4+ of 6 runs. If not, cores read as worse than bare.
- P2 **The hands differ:** Wake runs move 80%+ of fight time. Breaker runs keep a median 3.5 u or less to the nearest
  awake body. Bare runs sit between. Needs log fields: seconds moving, distance-to-nearest samples.
- P3 **The chain fires:** with a core and 1+ fit worn, a median of 1+ paid hit per pack fight, cashed within 2 s of
  the state going on.
- P4 **Fit matters, but isn't automatic:** when a fitting part stands on a pedestal, it is taken 50-80% of the time
  (above 80%, the pick is a formality, rule 6).
- P5 **Nothing broke:** hpLost d1-3 within 25% of TRIAL-1 (130). Pushes a fight 0.4-0.8.

**His feel, three questions, before the log:**
1. "Did the run with a core feel different under your thumbs than the part before it?" (PLACEHOLDER)
2. "At depth 4, what were you looking for?" A pass is a role or a part ("something for the sentinel"). A fail is
   "something stronger".
3. "When you took the core, did it feel like giving something up?" (PLACEHOLDER)

**The sim can answer:** core and fit offer rates, formation (core + 2 fits by d3: committed 70%+, random 30% or
less), take-rate shape. A headless Wake loop and Breaker stand against 3 hulks: is the state on within 1 s and
cashed inside a 4-6 s fight?

**The sim can't answer:** whether turning back to cut is fun on a thumb, whether the ribbon reads at 0.7 zoom on the
Poco, whether a snap reads as his doing or the game's, whether constant looping tires him, whether he sees the
pedestal glow from the beam.
