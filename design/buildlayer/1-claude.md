# Build layer, round 1: Claude (lead)

Written alone, before reading the other three. Every name here is PLACEHOLDER.

## The diagnosis

His verdict has one root: **every Still plays the same, so nothing a part does can make a run feel like a build.**

- The base kit decides how you fight, and it is the same for everyone: walk up and the hand hits, stand still and the eye
  shoots. Both are "damage the nearest thing", at 2.9 u and 11 u. A leaning is only "which of the two do you favour", so it
  feels forced: you're picking a range, not a way to play.
- The parts are all "a button that hurts things in a shape" (a cone, a line, a ring, a lob). Different shapes of the same
  verb. That's why they feel alike and why there feel like so few of them: 30 parts, maybe five distinct ideas.
- The combos we built are numbers (chilled/marked x2, riders that refill a button). The synergy round's own sim found a
  pair worth almost nothing on packs: a 4-6 s fight is over before a setup pays. A combo you can't feel isn't a combo.
- A run is ~14.5 min of play. Fewer offers, fewer swaps, less time for anything to come together.

So more parts, or louder parts, won't fix it. **The build has to change the verb.**

## 1. What a build is: a core and the parts that feed it

The unit a player commits to is **the core**: the thing at Still's centre that replaces both the hand and the eye. Each
core is a different verb, so it decides where you stand and when you move. Every core leaves something behind **in the
world**, a condition you can see on the floor or on a body. Parts pay off those conditions or change them.

That's the chain he asked for: the core sets it up, the parts cash it in, and every part you don't have yet is a "missing
piece" you can name ("I need something that pays slams").

Four cores, two of them in the first trial:

| core (PLACEHOLDER) | the verb | where you stand | what it leaves |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Ram** | a heavy shove, not a hit | in the crowd, back to a wall | **slammed**: a body knocked into a wall or another body, pinned for a beat |
| **Trail** | no attack while still; moving lays cold track | never still, circling | **frost**: track on the floor; a body that crosses it is chilled |
| **Hook** | pulls one body to you, then the next | the middle, gathering | **clump**: bodies dragged together, held a beat |
| **Lens** | planted shots (today's eye, kept as one core of four) | planted behind cover | **marked**: the shot's target, paid by the next hit |

A ram build wants walls and crowds. A trail build wants open floor and never stops. Those are different games in the hands,
not different numbers, and that's his "how you fight".

**Scrap, shared by every core.** Kills drop scrap on the floor (Still is built by what it survives). Some parts spend scrap
in reach (throw it, eat it for a shield, melt it into a cooldown). It pulls you across the floor after a kill, so even a
planted build moves, and it gives every core a second thing to plan around.

## 2. The pieces

- **Parts have a role:** a **payer** (cashes a condition: "hits a slammed body for triple and frees it"), a **shaper**
  (changes the condition: "frost track lasts twice as long, but only behind you"), or **plain** (works for anyone, weaker
  than a payer in its build). Every part names its condition on the card. A payer off its build still works, at plain value.
- **Conditions are made all the time by the core**, so a payer is live in the first second of a fight. That's what fixes
  "pairs are worth nothing on packs": the setup is free, and the part is the payoff.
- **Pool:** ~8 parts per core (payers + shapers across the four slots), ~10 plain, ~6 scrap parts. ~50 at four cores,
  ~32 for the two-core trial. A run should offer ~20 and you wear 4, so you never see everything (rule 9).
- **How it forms:** pick a core at the start, from two (cores unlock across runs, which is "wider, not stronger"). By d2 you
  hold one payer; by d3 two; by d6 the build is shaped and you're hunting the last piece. Pedestals lean toward your core.
- **The missing piece:** a drop card says "pays slammed" and lights if your core makes slams. A compare screen says what
  it would chain with. Nothing on the HUD.
- **Slots stay**, with a meaning: legs move you, arms hit, torso holds the area, head reaches. A core's payers spread across
  the slots, so no slot is dead in any build.

## 3. How you fight

- The **four buttons** are the build's payers and shapers, fired into conditions the core is making. A button is strongest
  when you've lined it up: a slam pinned on a wall, a clump gathered, a frost loop closed behind a chaser.
- **Tap push stays** (strain untouched, his call). A push now means "the condition is up and the button isn't: pay it now".
- What disappears: two universal autos that make every Still the same. One core per run is the auto.

## 4. Keep / cut / reshape

| system | call | why |
|---|---|---|
| four slots, part = button | **keep** | the body fantasy; the slot gives a payer its shape |
| hand and eye | **reshape** | the eye becomes the Lens core; the hand's break folds into Ram (a shove breaks a windup) |
| leanings (tags) | **cut** | the core is the leaning, and it changes how you play instead of labelling it |
| riders | **cut** | refilling a button is a number; payers do the job |
| states | **reshape** | chilled and marked become two cores' conditions; slammed and hauled (designed, never built) are Ram's and Hook's |
| temper / melt | **keep, narrowed** | ranks are inside-run growth; rank III adds a second condition the part answers, not a bigger number |
| mastery | **cut** | it was "change your auto"; the core is that now |
| pedestals | **keep** | the place your core brings payer offers |
| weight's numbers | **keep** | the heavier feel fits a payer landing |
| strain / tap push | **keep** | his call |

## 5. The first trial

Two cores, **Ram** and **Trail**: the furthest apart in the hands, and both new (Lens is close to today, so it can't show
the difference). Each gets ~8 parts; ~8 of today's parts stay as plain; scrap with 3 parts. The core picked at the run's
start. Today's hand/eye kit stays behind a pause switch for comparison.

**Pass lines.** His feel first: by d3, can he say in one sentence what his build is, and does a Ram run feel like a
different game from a Trail run? Then the log:
- payers' share of part damage >= 50% in a formed build (paying their condition, not plain hits);
- a core-matching part is taken >= 2x as often as an off-core one when offered;
- where he stands differs: Trail runs moving >= 70% of fight time; Ram runs within 2 u of a wall or a body >= 50%;
- no part taken >= 70% of >= 6 offers (rule 6); every slot has a payer for each core (rule 5).

## Where I disagree with the brief or him

- **The run is too short for a build to form**, and that's not a later problem. ~1.6 min a level gives maybe 12 offers a run.
  The balancer should say how many a build needs; if it's ~20, levels need to be longer or richer *in this round's trial*,
  not in the threat round.
- **Picking a core at the start is less emergent than finding one.** I accept that for the trial: it makes "I have a build"
  true by d1, and a found core can come later.
- **Rule 3 (no multipliers between parts) stays.** A payer's triple is the part's own number, gated by a condition the
  enemy has to be in, which is rule 2.
