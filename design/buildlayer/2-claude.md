## Where I hold, or side

- **The balancer's temper finding is the most important thing in the round.** Four melts to III is +27-37% on packs and
  +68-84% on a boss; no chain at rank I comes close. Unflattened, the best build is "melt everything", and that is the
  deckbuilder's stat-stacking failure back again. I side with flattening (1.15 / 1.3 damage, 0.92 / 0.85 cooldown) in the
  trial itself, not later.
- **Spending is the bottleneck** (balancer). So the parts we hunt are payers, shapers and keystones, never "also sets the
  state". I side with cutting the feeder role.
- **x2 used up vs flat per mark:** I lean to the balancer's flat per mark. It can't be wasted on overkill, it rewards what
  the core did, and it keeps rule 3 clean. The translator's x2 reads more simply; round 2 should settle it on the
  button: the lit glyph means "now" either way.
- **The bare kit stays as the control** (translator), behind a switch that is **on by default** (verifier). He plays
  the live game and doesn't want finished work hidden.
- **Measure first** (verifier's B0): the drop simulator and the check suites have to describe the game he plays before
  any build number counts. That's stage zero, not a side task.
- **Rule 9 needs designing, not more content** (balancer): 25-40% of committed runs take nothing after d6. The core's own
  upgrades (mastery reshaped, at most 3 a run) and a second keystone per core (one for packs, one for bosses, a swap
  before the Engine) are the late want.
