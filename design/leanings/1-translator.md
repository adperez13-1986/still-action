# Round 1: the translator (how the two leanings play on a phone)

## The frame: two verbs, taught by the autos, paid by the parts
A leaning is only real if it's something the player *does differently* with their thumbs.
Today it already is. **Close is the left thumb pressing in**: you step into the windup and the
hand strikes (ring on the floor). **Marksman is the left thumb lifting off**: 0.3 s later the
sightline appears and the eye fires. That split is the best thing we have, so the leanings
should grow out of it, not sit on top of it as tags.

So each leaning gets **one verb the base kit teaches before any part mentions it**, and parts
pay on that verb:
- **Break** (close): the hand's strike lands in a windup and shatters it. Exists (~1 strike in 5,
  ~38 a run). Ember shatter, the existing interrupt sound.
- **Pin** (marksman, new): a *steady* planted shot lands in a windup. The body's windup is cut
  and it's nailed where it stands (0.8 s, can't walk, open x1.5, like a reel). The look: a cold
  spike from the shot into the floor under it and a frost ring. Cold where the break is ember, so
  the two can't be confused in a crowd.

This settles the tension the brief names. A set bonus has no *moment*: it's on or off, with nothing
on screen at the instant it matters, which is exactly why it switches on by accident. A payoff on a
verb has a place (the body that broke or pinned) and a button (the part it fed). On a 6-inch screen,
"that hulk shattered and my dash button snapped full" teaches itself. "You have 3 close parts" doesn't.

## Making marksman real: steady, shown in the world
His words were "having to stay still feels like I have to absorb blows" and "looks strong but is
weak". Both are readability problems as much as numbers: the plant costs something you can see
(you stop dodging) and pays something you can't (a halved blow you may never take).
- **Steady:** consecutive planted shots at the same body step up. Shot 1 is today's (5). From shot
  3, a shot is steady: more damage (balancer's call; I'd start at 9), and it pins a windup. The
  eye's own ramp, not a bonus between parts (rule 3).
- **The sightline is the meter.** Dashed on shot 1, tighter on 2, solid and brighter at steady,
  with one soft rising tone per step. Step, change target or let the hand take over and it snaps
  back to dashed with a dry tick: *you can see what you just gave up*. No HUD element at all.
- **The eye's floor mark.** The hand has its ring; the eye gets one too: three short cold ticks
  under Still's feet at settle (his legs spread a touch). The brace is then visible *before* the
  blow, not only as a clang after it. That's the fix for "absorbing": it reads as bracing.
- **Target order:** a shooter mid-windup first (the swelling lens is the thing to pin), then
  elites, shooters, nearest. The sightline jumping to the locking sentinel *is* the warning.
- Timing: steady comes ~1.5 s after the thumb lifts (0.3 settle + 2 beats): a commitment, but
  reachable against a sentinel's 760 ms lock if you plant early. Needs his hands to confirm.

## Card and compare wording
- **Two lead-ins, always first, always bold:** "**On a break:**" and "**On a pin:**". A player
  reads the leaning off the first two words; the rest of the line is the behaviour. Parts that
  lean without paying (sources, defence, mobility) carry only the glyph.
- **Glyphs drawn from the world marks:** close = a short arc of the hand's ring; marksman = a
  dashed line ending in a dot (the sightline). Neutral: no glyph. On the card tag, the compare
  screen, the pedestal plinth and the wall plaque. **Never on the HUD or the buttons.**
- **Rule of legibility:** a player should guess a part's glyph from its card line alone. If they
  can't, the tag or the line is wrong. ("Shoves enemies away" = marksman; "yanks enemies to you" =
  close.) I'd run every tag past that test before shipping.
- **Compare screen, the loss made real:** under the two cards, a strip of Still's four slot glyphs,
  now and after: `[arc][arc][dot][ - ]  ->  [arc][dot][dot][ - ]`, with one line: "Your next
  pedestals will lean marksman." That's the true consequence (steering), not a stat. If the part
  going back has a payoff, add: "You'd give up: On a break, Kickstart is ready again."
- A fourth stat row on payoff parts only (`pays on  break` / `pin`): the payoff is the point.
- **Avoid "leader"** on any card: the game shows elites (aura, name), never the word. Triggers on
  windups are visible; triggers on categories have to be learned.

## Example payoffs, as the player meets them (numbers are the balancer's)
| part | lean | card line | reads on screen as |
|---|---|---|---|
| Kickstart (legs, white) | close | Dash, running over anything. **On a break:** it's ready again. | shatter, legs button snaps full with a click: dash to the next body |
| Parry Clamp (arms) | close | stays the break *source* (the snap that makes breaks) | the thing that feeds the others; no lead-in |
| Patient Lens (head) | marksman | Charges between shots. **On a pin:** fully charged. | spike, lens button fills: fire the big shot at the pinned body |
| Skitter (legs, white) | marksman | A quick hop. **On a pin:** it's ready again. | hop to a new plant; steady restarts, a real trade |
Payoffs that give *tempo* (a button back) read best on a phone: the reward lands where the thumb
already is. Avoid payoffs that give invisible numbers (+damage for 3 s). Cap refunds (one per ~4 s)
so a break storm doesn't turn a button into a second auto.

## Per slot, the gaps as they read
Tagging by card line gives: head 5 marksman, 0 close (Coil and Signal Flare could lean close, both
rare/off the starter pool); arms 5 close, 1-2 marksman (Clamp Toss throws them off you; Piston's
knockback is ambiguous: pick close and say "up close" on the card); torso marksman-heavy (Vent,
Ward, Mirror, Lure) vs close (Backdraft, Brace); legs even. **Two holes: head-close and
arms-marksman**, and the starting whites must split roughly 3/3/2 or run one leans marksman by
accident. Two new parts, mirrored, would close both and each read its leaning from its line:
- **Welding Eye** (head, blue, close): "A short hot beam at whatever your hand is on." Ends if you
  leave reach. A head part that lives inside the ring.
- **Kickstand** (arms, blue, marksman): "Drives an arm into the floor. Until you walk out of its
  circle, you stay planted." Gives up a swing for the freedom to step without losing steady. A
  visible prop plus a circle: the marksman's dodge.

## The pedestal match
- **Nothing tagged worn** (run start, or only neutrals): no match. The three pedestals show **one
  close, one marksman, one anything**: the first pedestal is a fork, and it teaches both glyphs.
- **Wearing tags:** one of three matches the leaning you wear more of; a tie goes to the most
  recently taken part. Its plinth glyph is lit cold; the other two are carved but dark.
- **Walk up and the card says why:** "leans close, like your Kickstart and Cleaver." Naming the
  worn parts is the only explanation the match needs.
- Flag: the plinth glyph must read at the drift-in zoom (1.1) on the Poco, in the dim grade. It
  may need to be projected on the floor in front of the pedestal instead of carved on it.

## Things that read badly, flagged
1. Planted with a hulk in reach, the hand wins the auto. The sightline dropping to the ring must
   be instant and obvious, or steady will feel stolen.
2. Pins must be about as frequent as breaks (target 0.5-1.5x), or every marksman payoff feels dead.
3. Frayed Cleaver keys on strain, not an enemy: close, but no payoff. No lead-in.
4. Auto-firing payoffs (a Ward that opens itself on a lock) are tempting for thumbs but the button
   starts cooling without a press. If tried, the button needs its own "it fired" pulse.
5. The retreat rule (no strike backing off) is invisible: grey the ring for those frames.

## Guardrails (log per run)
Matched pedestal taken 40-65% (over 75 = always-take, under 30 = match means nothing); loadouts
ending with 3+ of one glyph 45-65%, mixed runs finishing about as often; each payoff part fires 3+
times per depth worn (else dead); pins vs breaks 0.5-1.5x; eye shots vs strikes from 1:2 toward
1:1.3; planted time share up. Log `pins`, `steadyShots`, `payoffs[id]`, `pedestalMatch` per depth.

## Recommended leaning design
- Two verbs, taught by the base kit: **break** (hand, exists) and **pin** (a steady planted shot in a windup, new).
- Payoffs live on single parts and fire on those verbs; no set bonuses, no counting worn tags.
- The eye steadies: 3 shots on one body, then more damage and pins; the sightline dashed -> solid is the only meter.
- The eye gets a floor mark (three ticks, legs spread) so the brace shows before the blow.
- Cards lead with **On a break:** / **On a pin:**; glyphs (ring arc, dashed dot) on card, compare, plinth, wall. Never HUD.
- Payoffs give tempo (a button snaps full), capped; no hidden +damage windows.
- Compare screen shows the four-glyph strip now -> after and "Your next pedestals will lean ...", plus what payoff you'd give up.
- Two new mirrored parts close the only holes: Welding Eye (head, close) and Kickstand (arms, marksman).
- Starting whites split ~3 close / 3 marksman / 2 neutral, so run one doesn't lean by accident.
- Pedestals: nothing worn = a fork (one of each + one free); otherwise one lit match, named on the card.
- Ship as one trial: tags + glyphs, steady/pin, ~4 payoff reworks, 2 new parts, the log counters.
