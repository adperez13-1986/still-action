# Round 2: Claude

## Where I changed my mind

- **Cracks belong to the bosses, not the crawl.** The balancer's window table
  settles it: a hulk's 760 ms recover already lands about 34 damage with no push,
  against 30 HP. Fodder dies inside its own openings, so any crawl opening that
  pays in damage (my C-D1 on the hulk, the translator's "a miss is an opening")
  asks for nothing. The balancer's boss cracks (a damage line inside one opening,
  crossing it removes a move) are my C-D1 done properly. I drop mine for theirs.
- **Drop my refund (C-R2).** The balancer shows that Stopped already swings from
  14% to 73% on half a push a fight. A refund for well-read pushes makes that
  worse, and the free band already exists: it just needs drawing (the verifier's
  E2, the balancer's T2, the translator's E1 are all the same idea).
- **"A push breaks a wind-up" is smaller than I thought.** The verifier's 430 ms
  table: it answers the sentinel (760 ms), the ram only from the tracking rails,
  the Lobber barely, and the hulk only with instant parts. Keep it, but as a
  rule that pays sometimes, not as the fix.

## What the four of us agree on, without saying so

The crawl can't be made to *need* a push through damage or danger: walking
answers every threat (verifier), fodder dies in its windows and HP comes back
(balancer). So crawl demand has to come from one of two places:
- **timing aimed at you:** the balancer's D2, elites that commit when your answer
  is dark;
- **a different verb:** pushed halves that are other moves (the balancer's R2,
  the verifier's R3). These make the push a choice of *what*, which neither
  cooldowns nor walking can supply.

Bosses are where a push can buy something at a fair price, because nothing comes
back until the end. That's the balancer's budget argument, and I agree with it.

## Legibility goes first, as its own step

Three voices found the same thing from different sides: a cooling button looks
switched off (translator), a hold is silent until it fires, and a tap on a cooling
button is thrown away unrecorded (verifier). Only 2 of 30 parts ever hint, and
neither is in the starting pool (balancer). Before any new rule, fix what the
screen says, and **count dead taps**. After three runs on the Poco, `deadTaps`
tells us whether the thought comes up once the button stops looking dead. If it
does, some of the demand work may be smaller than we think.
