# Round 2: Translator (feel, UX, emotion)

## (a) The three I'd back most

1. **Put back together** (Verifier W3 and Claude C-W2). Getting from the
   ending to home is the reassembly: Broken comes home in pieces and is
   rebuilt, and Stopped comes home dim and his eye lights again. It's cheap
   because the rig exists, and it keeps the two endings different all the way
   to the door.
2. **A run is one day** (Claude C-R1). Morning, afternoon, dusk, then night
   for the walk home, so depth is time you can feel without a number. It beats
   my W6 (the phone's clock), which I withdraw: the two conflict, and C-R1 is
   the one with meaning.
3. **The doorframe** (Verifier W4). The kids' height marks grow with the
   calendar. It's genesis's "what my kids will develop into" shown as the one
   thing in the room that moves, and you can't grind for it.

## (b) What feels wrong

- **The last Assembler fights with your discards** (Balancer R6). It turns
  letting go into a threat you'll face later. Moth and flame should cost you
  the familiar part, not punish you for leaving it. It's also big to build.
- **"You got this far last time"** (Verifier R4 banner). That's a high-score
  line. And a dark, slumped Still every run will read as a corpse on a small
  screen. Keep a marker (my cairn), and drop the words and the body.
- **Twenty hooks** (Balancer M4). A forced meta trade on a pickup card, in
  the middle of a crawl, is a spreadsheet moment. Curation belongs in the
  Workshop (Verifier M3, my M8), between runs.
- **Endless past depth 6** (Balancer R3). A run needs an end you can see.
  Endless turns "enough" into "until you fail".

## (c) The open disagreements

| question | my position |
|---|---|
| Kids: first night, or runs 3 and 6 | **First night.** An empty bench for three runs is absence you earn back by attending. Separate their presence from their parts: they're home from the first homecoming, and *their parts* can join the pool at runs 3 and 6 if you want that pacing. |
| Run length | **6 depths** (~26 min, one phone sitting). A warm beam after Assembler I (all four of us converged on it). After Assembler II, a **walk home, not a boss**: a short approach to the lit house (Claude C-R2, my R3). The 6-depth run gets fewer drops, so pair it with the 12-part starter pool. |
| Wear strain floor | **Yes to the floor, with two changes.** Wear only from choices you make (Plenty, a Den), never from beating a boss, because success shouldn't cost you. And make it visible: a darker section at the bottom of the strain bar that quiets don't drain, landing with its own sound (a click that doesn't come back). An invisible second number fails the "feel the commitment" test. Sim it first. |
| Sit down; Broken losing that depth's parts | **Sit down: yes.** On a phone it's the graceful way to end a run for the night: a real Stopped, "gave what it had", with no quit button. **Broken losing parts: no.** Genesis says you never leave with nothing, and a loss on the sudden ending makes it the buzzer. Keep the endings different in look and sound. Every ending keeps everything, and "glimpsed" parts stay found. |
| Kids draw your run, or the engine snapshot | **Both, as one thing.** The snapshot is the source and the crayon shader is the hand. A kid redraws the frame the run ended on and pins it up. That needs no "defining thing" picker (the risky part of C-W1), and the drawing is always true to the run. |

## (d) One combined pitch per topic

**Workshop: Coming home.** The ending plays as a reassembly under Grace's
light (V-W3, C-W2). Then you're in a small iso room you walk around (all
three W1s). The window shows the hour the run ended (C-R1). Yanah and Yuri
are there from the first night (mine W3), and the doorframe marks grow with
the calendar (V-W4). The newest drawing of the run goes up on the wall
(C-W1 over my W4). On the way to the cold-beam door, the wall of parts
(B-W1, my M1) lets you choose the one white Still starts with (V-W3, my M2)
and turn parts you don't want to face the wall (V-M3). No menus, and
there's always a door about four seconds away. *Medium.*

**Meta: Wider you can see, a past you can read.** A 12-part starter pool
opens toward 30 through an unfound channel (B-M1, V-M1), keyed so the fastest
way to find parts is a full run. Found parts hang on the wall. Parts remember
the runs they were on, as a grey line and a name (V-M2, C-M2, my M4). Node
kinds open once, from moments rather than currency (B-M5, V-M4), and stay
blank until you've met them (my M5). The room fills a little per run, and
Still gets patched, never stronger (my M3, M6). *Cheap to medium.*

**Run: One day, two areas, home.** 6 depths, morning to dusk (C-R1, V-R1,
B-R2). You choose a route by walking into one of the exit beams, each with
a floor glyph (my R4, V-R3). Each has two node kinds: a Den for growth and a
Hollow for comfort (B-R4). After Assembler I, a warm beam home (V-R2, B-R3,
my R2). After Assembler II, the night walk up to the lit house (C-R2).
**Sit down** at a Rest shrine is a chosen Stopped. Wear stays visible if the
sim needs it. Three endings, everything kept. Build "The Warm Beam"
(Verifier) first. *Cheap first, medium in full.*

## Summary

1. Back: reassembly as the way home, a run as one day (replacing my phone-clock idea), and the doorframe height marks.
2. Against: the Assembler wearing your discards, the "got this far" banner and shell, twenty hooks mid-crawl, and endless depth.
3. Kids home from the first night, with only their parts paced by run count; 6 depths, warm beam after boss I, a walk to the lit house instead of a final boss.
4. Wear only from growth choices and shown as a stain on the meter; Sit down yes; Broken keeps everything, so the endings differ in look and sound, not loot.
5. The kids redraw the engine's ending frame in crayon: one keepsake that is both true and theirs.
