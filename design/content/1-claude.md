# Round 1: Claude's pitches (the fourth voice)

## Second boss

**C-B1. The Pair.** Area II's boss is two machines, not one, with half the
Assembler's HP each: one that holds the middle and sweeps, and one that keeps
its distance and fires. Kill one and the other takes over one of its moves,
so the order you kill them in changes the second half of the fight. It tests
target choice and positioning, which the Assembler (one big body) never
asks for, and it gives Lure, Clamp Toss and Mirror Ward a boss where they
matter. *Cost:* medium (two smaller rigs, mostly reusing existing move code).

**C-B2. Lights out** *(surprising)*. It's dusk, and the boss arena is where
the day ends. As the fight goes on, the fog closes in and the arena darkens,
until the only things lit are Grace's light around Still and the boss's own
ember tells. By the last phase you read the fight from the telegraphs alone,
and they were built to be readable on their own. The boss itself can be simple
(the Assembler's cousin). What makes it new is the light. *Cost:* cheap for
the light, but readability on the phone is the risk to test.

## Second environment

**C-E1. The quarter where people lived** *(surprising)*. Area II is the ruins of
a residential quarter, not more dungeon. Cover is broken furniture, doorways,
low garden walls, a cot. It gets more intact the deeper you go: depth 4 is
rubble, depth 5 has roofs half on, and by the boss the houses almost stand.
It's the road home. The night walk at the end reaches the one house still
lit. It's the same waist-high rule and the same grade, but a different
material (timber, plaster, tile) and a different sound (wood creaks, a
curtain in the draft, a far-off dog). *Cost:* medium (a CC0 furniture and
house kit if one fits; triplanar textures carry the rest).

## Enemy variety

**C-V1. Named ones, from the old roster.** Each level has one named enemy
from the original deckbuilder's 43 (Rust Guard, Fracture Mite, Echo
Construct...). It's an existing archetype with one small twist tied to its
name, never more HP. The name shows over it the way elites' do, and it gets a
notebook page. That's 43 small variations for the cost of data and a twist
table, and it carries the lineage. *Cost:* cheap per name, but it needs
writing 43 twists (or fewer, say 12, and let the rest come later).

**C-V2. The crate that isn't** *(cheap)*. One crate in a level is a sleeping
enemy, with a faint ember seam along its lid if you look. Smash it and it
unfolds. It punishes breaking everything on reflex, and it's fair because the
tell is there before you swing. It teaches players to read the room.
