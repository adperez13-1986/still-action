# Round 1: translator

Lens: the player over many runs. Why someone opens it for run 20 on a bus, what a choice
feels like under a thumb, how the Workshop, the family and the endings carry a run into the
next one, which content earns its cost, and when discovery hands over to mastery.

## 1. The direction: choices in the run, questions in the house

I agree with Claude's split and want to move where the line sits. Hours of distinct play
is the balancer's number. The number I care about is **why the next run starts**. A player
starts run 20 because a question is open: a build they haven't tried, a road they haven't
taken, something at home that asked. Systems give runs variety. Only the house asks
questions. Today it asks one (fill the wall) and answers it in ~6 runs.

| | systems (choices) | content (areas, bosses, story) |
|---|---|---|
| cost for ~1 h of new play | the drop choice and the ladder: ~1 evening an hour | the Line, stages A-C: ~14 evenings for ~1.5 h, so ~9 evenings an hour |
| what it gives run 20 | a different build, a harder day | a different place, something new to see |
| what wears out | the choice goes rote once it's solved (a ladder rung, a best pick) | the place runs out of surprise (~3 visits) |
| what only it can do | mastery | meaning, and a reason to go and look |

**Target:** ~15-20 h of distinct play, about 40 runs, then a game you come back to as a
favourite (premium allows that; it doesn't need to go on forever). Discovery runs 1-12 (the
wall, the notebook, the road, the first wishes), mastery runs 10-40 (weather, the same day
again, named builds). The handover has to be a moment you see in the room, not a quiet
drop-off when the last plaque lights.

**On a phone, the sitting is a depth, not a run.** A run is 26 min, and you resume at a beam.
So the real unit is a 4-5 min depth. Every depth needs one decision you remember at the next
beam, or the run is six sittings of the same thing.

## 2. What a meaningful choice is at thumb scale

- **Made by walking, never a menu.** The Workshop already does this (walk up to things), and
  the beams do too. Run decisions stay walk-into: pedestals, shrines, beams, the hook.
- **The loss is visible where the choice is made.** The old part already drops at your feet:
  that's the right instinct. But a swap can be undone until you leave the level, so it weighs
  nothing. A choice counts when what you didn't take goes somewhere you can see (back to the
  wall, dark on its pedestal), never into a stat line.
- **One real decision per depth**, about 8-10 a run, each with a named loss. Today the run
  has about three (the warm beam, shrines, the first 2-3 drops), and 60% of early drops fill
  an empty slot (`FILL_EMPTY_CHANCE`), so the buttons are all lit by depth 2 and the run's
  best arc (sparse to whole) is over in 8 minutes.
- **Nothing that stops a fight.** No pick-three mid-crawl. The compare pause is the most a
  fight can take.

## 4. The three endings between runs

They already look and sound distinct. What's missing is that each one leaves something
different in the house, without paying more:
- **Home** leaves the hour (afternoon, the kids awake; night, asleep) and ticks a weather rung.
- **Stopped** leaves the dimmed eye coming back on. Its card gets the strain line drawn to
  the edge, and the child draws Still sitting down, not falling.
- **Broken** leaves the reassembly on the bench. Its card is drawn in pieces, taped together.
The kids draw the ending, not a grade. None of them should look like the lesser card on
the board.

## 5. What to stop, or not build

- **Pick-one-of-three at every drop.** Mid-fight menus on a phone. Only at bosses and Plenty.
- **A collection %, achievements, run score, dailies, streaks.** The wall is the collection,
  and the corkboard is the history.
- **A fourth area** (the old town stays parked) before moves 1-3 are played.
- **Authored story: cutscenes, dialogue, lore text.** Story comes from the house changing:
  the hour, the kids' traces, notebook pages in Adrian's hand. His words are the most
  expensive content in the game. Spend them on the three endings, ~20 notebook pages and
  the wishes.
- **The kids as figures** (settled as traces; keep it, since traces age better over 40 runs).
- **More parts past ~36 before the pedestals exist.** Without a choice, more parts only
  thin out what falls.

## 6. The surprising one: wishes on the corkboard

One crayon card pinned at a time, in the corner of the board, drawn by Yanah or Yuri
(whoever didn't draw the last run). It's a wish to **see** something, never a task to beat:
*the big train. a ram asleep. the Arbiter's lamp. come home while we're awake. a part with a
name. rain.* You see it in a run, and that child draws it on the run's card in place of the
last frame; the wish goes into a shoebox under the board that you can walk up to and leaf
through. Then another is pinned. A wish never expires, never nags, never shows
disappointment, and nothing unlocks from it.

Why it's the move: it's what makes content worth its cost. Each new area, family, boss or
weather adds a wish, so building content grows the reasons to look, and those reasons come
from the family, as warmth, not as a stat. It gives run 20 an open question that isn't
"do better", and it hands discovery to mastery gently (the later wishes need weather or a
road: *come home in the storm*).

The idea no document has: **the wishes could be drawn by Yanah and Yuri themselves.** An
afternoon at the kitchen table, twenty drawings, scanned. The cost is paper. It's the one
piece of content no one else could put in this game. (Whether his kids' drawings ship in a
public game is his call, and it can wait until the game ships.)

Cost: medium. Detection hooks for ~20 sights, a card slot on the board, the shoebox, and the
crayon pipeline that's already built.

## Recommendation

**Choices in the run, questions in the house.** Systems carry the depth (a named loss at
every decision, walk-into, one per depth). Content is chosen by one test: it must ask a
question the house can hold (a wish, a notebook page, a road). Target 15-20 h over ~40 runs.

**First three moves:**
1. **The fourth button lights at the Assembler** (fill chance 0.6 to ~0.3). One evening; it
   brings back the start-incomplete arc in every run.
2. **The boss's gift on three pedestals**, the two you leave go back to the wall. 1-2 evenings.
3. **Wishes on the corkboard**, starting with 8 sights already in the game. 2 evenings, plus
   the drawings, which are his.
