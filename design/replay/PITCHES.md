# Pitches: where still-action's replayability comes from

26 Sep 2026. Four voices (balancer, translator, verifier, me), two rounds; raw rounds in this
folder, brief in `BRIEF.md`. A direction and an order, not a spec. Nothing built.

---

## The answer: choices for depth, content where it multiplies

All four voices, both rounds. The numbers behind it (the balancer's simulation of the drop code):

- **Today:** about 7 real decisions a run (one every ~4 min). Of ~25 drops, ~2 touch the build
  you're after. A build you aim for at the start ends up worn in **27%** of runs. The 3,136
  possible loadouts aren't depth: parts just add up (only ~7% of cross-slot pairs interact).
- **Effort:** systems buy ~1.1 h of distinct play per evening of building; content ~0.2 h. Content
  climbs to ~1 h per evening only once builds can be steered, because then a new road multiplies
  builds instead of adding to them.
- **Where it gets to:** ~3.5 h of distinct play today, 16-22 h with the hybrid below (about 30
  evenings). Diablo II-style volume would be 100+ evenings.

So: steer builds first, then add content that multiplies them. Content keeps its job as heart and
character: the Line to stage B, bosses that answer you, the Workshop, the family.

## Your play time is the budget

The verifier's point, which all four adopted: one tester can judge *feel*, not rates. The plan owes
about 29 of your runs (~12.5 h). So:
- **At most one combat change and one economy change on trial at a time.**
- **First, close the switches that are open:** the hand and the eye are both new and both on. Play
  three runs (hand only, eye only, both), then keep what you'd miss.
- **This is the last pitch round for a while.** Build, play, then design again.

## The moves, in order

**1. Counters and a drop simulator** (1 evening, costs none of your play). The playtest log records
which parts were offered, taken and left; the balancer's simulator lives in the repo so drop odds
are tuned by script, not by your runs.

**2. Picks on pedestals** (about 2 evenings). Three parts rise on pedestals you walk into, and the
other two go back to the wall:
- **beside each crawl level's exit beam** (depths 1, 2, 4, 5): the pick before you leave;
- **at the Assembler and at Plenty.**
Not at elites (a pick mid-fight, and it fights the thief) and not only at bosses (the Arbiter's gift
lands after the run's last fight). **Empty slots fill only from pedestals**, so your fourth button
lights at the Assembler and the run has an arc from sparse to whole. (Lowering the fill chance does
nothing: an empty slot's drop gets taken anyway.) A chosen build forms in **~59%** of runs, from 27%.
- **A second pick at the Assembler for +4 strain that doesn't wear off** (the balancer's merge of
  "push the drop"): wanting more costs strain, and Stopped becomes the result of wanting too much.
  Plenty already sells a part for strain, so it's in the spirit of the rules. **Your call.**

**3. Leaning tags, and one pedestal that matches you** (1 evening). Tags use the variety round's
names (close, marksman, caster, keeper), shown on cards and the wall. In every set of three, one
pedestal matches a leaning you already wear: visible steering, nothing hidden.

**4. Wishes on the corkboard** (3 evenings, with save v4). The kids pin crayon wishes: *see the big
train, find a ram asleep, come home while we're awake.* Seeing one in a run puts it on that run's
card. Wishes never expire, never nag, unlock nothing, and every new area, enemy family or boss adds
one, which is what makes new content worth building. **Whether Yanah and Yuri draw them for real is
yours alone.** Save v4 also stores each part's history by name, so the next boss doesn't need a risky
migration.

**5. Enemy families, then the Mirrored champion** (about 4.5 evenings): enemies that ask for answers
matter once you can steer a build to answer them.

**6. A rule ladder of 6 rungs** (about 4.5 evenings with seeds): opt-in harder days, each rung a rule
that breaks one build style, never bigger numbers. Seeds let you retry a day.

**7. The Line to stage B** (about 9.5 evenings): the second road.

**8. The maze remembers the hook** (the verifier's surprising idea): the next run's first boss wears
the part you left on the hook and changes its moves to counter it. The balancer counts it as turning
2 boss fights into 8; the translator warns it turns the one part you carry from home against you, and
would make it an opt-in rung. **Your call:** default, or a ladder rung.
