# Round 2: Translator

Step 1 is assumed built. That undoes my round-1 case: I picked the old town
because taking walking away was the one way to make pushes wanted without
step 1. With it, the yard's opportunities are enough, on the game's most
proven tell (lanes).

## Where I changed my mind

- **Placement: the alternate, not my branch.** The balancer is right: a
  second cold beam is a menu with no cost that dilutes noon's one real
  decision. Three beams in one yard is also my pitch's worst phone read. I
  keep one thing: **the cold beam wears its road** (a `wall_gated` frame for
  the Works; rails running in and a far horn for the yard), so "go on or go
  home" is decided knowing what's next ("the yard today, at 8 strain"). Run 1
  is always II (verifier); turns alternate among runs reaching depth 4
  (balancer). *Flag for Adrian:* the branch is half an evening on top later.
- **The old town is parked, not merged.** A stroke that hits what moves, on
  top of a timetable, is two clocks in one level fighting over the left
  thumb: "stand still" against "get off the rails". The stillness rule needs
  its own place, as a later door; the Watcher, tick mites, Winder and Hour
  go with it.
- **The departure board survives, showing where, not which slot.** A named
  slot is one more mid-fight read, and step 1 gives push demand enough.

## The yard, as it reads on a 6-inch phone

- **Rails vs bodies.** Round 1 put lanes everywhere (trains, rams, Handcar,
  Rake), so one visual rule: **a train lights the two rails**, two ember
  lines with sleeper glints over dark ballast, while a body's rush fills a
  strip, as the ram's does. At 16 px/u the rails are 22 px apart and read as
  "the track", not "a charge". A ram on rails blurs the two, so the Handcar
  and the Rake are cut.
- **Timing.** A crossing bell 2.5 s out (sound and rail hum only, not
  committed), then the rails light 1.3 s before the train (committed, and
  booked 300 ms from other locks, as the verifier says). The signal lamp is
  0.3 u (5 px), so it's dressing; the rails are the tell.
- **Numbers: the balancer's.** 22 damage and a shove clear, every 16-22 s a
  line. A train that ploughs through a pack and leaves everything standing
  feels wrong. 22 kills anything that shoots and wrecks a hulk (leaves it at
  8), which reads as "the world is bigger than the fight". Two trains still
  can't do a fight for him, because nothing waits on the rails.
- **Enemies step off lit rails unless committed** (balancer), every kind,
  plus the verifier's brood no-go. A hulk walking into a visible train looks
  stupid; one that reared there, or that you shoved, looks like your doing.

## The monster set: three, plus one gated

1. **Signalman** (verifier M1, Claude's flagman, my semaphore). A semaphore
   arm rising over 900 ms is the tallest, clearest wind-up in the set, and
   it's where step 1 teaches itself. Creosoted timber, look-tested against
   the thief's rust.
2. **Sleepers** as a brood face (verifier M3, Claude's ballast crawlers).
   Mites asleep as the ties, embers in the gaps. A train never wakes them.
   It costs half an evening and brings no new read.
3. **Porter** (balancer's version). A cageless thief running a crate to a
   live rail. It's the only push in the game about loot, and it adds nothing
   to the combat read: a small body with a box, running.
4. **Linesman** (balancer's, or Claude's coupled pair), **only if** its wire
   (one sagging strip of shivering dust, never two lines) can't be mistaken
   for rails on the phone. Otherwise cut.

Cut: the Handcar and the Rake (lanes on lanes); the Winch stays parked.
